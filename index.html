What it means
These pages have a canonical tag pointing to a different URL. Google is respecting the canonical and indexing the canonical target, not these pages. Down from 520 → 139 (73% improvement) but 139 still represents significant indexable content being excluded.
Fix: Audit all 139 pages. Ensure canonical tags are intentional. If any pages are canonical-ing to wrong targets, fix the tag. If pages are duplicate, consolidate content.

Issue 2 — Crawled, Currently Not Indexed
Google-reported · 127 pages · RISING TREND ⚠
Critical
⚠
What it means — and why it's the #1 priority
Google is actively crawling these pages but choosing NOT to index them. This is a content quality signal. Google's algorithms are judging these pages as low-value, thin, duplicate-intent, or not meeting E-E-A-T standards. The count rose from 42 → 127 (+202%) — Google is getting more selective about your content over time.
Fix: Run a content audit on all 127 URLs. Check for thin content (<500 words), duplicate headings/meta, poor E-E-A-T, keyword stuffing, missing author/date signals, no unique value. Improve or merge/redirect weak pages. Submit improved pages via URL Inspection tool.
Impact: CriticalEffort: High127 URLs
Issue 3 — Not Found (404)
Website-reported · 54 pages · improving
Improving
✕
What it means
These URLs return a 404 response. They may have been deleted, moved without redirects, or were never live. Down from 123 → 54 — good progress. Remaining 54 should be resolved to recover any lost link equity and fix broken user journeys.
Fix: Export the 404 list from GSC. For each URL, either: (a) restore the page if valuable, (b) set up a 301 redirect to the most relevant live page, or (c) ensure they return true 404 (not soft-404). Update all internal links pointing to dead URLs.
Impact: MediumEffort: Low54 URLs
Issue 4 — Blocked by robots.txt
Website-reported · 60 pages · mostly stable
Stable
🤖
What it means
60 pages are disallowed by robots.txt rules. Most may be intentional (admin, staging, duplicate parameterized URLs). However there's a risk that important pages are accidentally blocked — especially if the number briefly spiked to 82 before settling at 60.
Fix: Review your robots.txt against these 60 URLs. Confirm each blocked page is intentionally excluded. Remove Disallow rules for any valuable indexable pages accidentally caught. Pay special attention to JS/CSS resources — blocking them can prevent Google rendering your pages correctly.
Impact: MediumEffort: Low60 URLs
Issue 5 — Excluded by Noindex Tag
Website-reported · 22 pages
Improving
🚫
What it means
22 pages have a noindex directive in their HTML or HTTP headers. Down from 35 → 22. These pages are explicitly excluded from Google's index. Review each to determine if the noindex was intentional or an error (e.g., left over from a staging deployment).
Fix: Check CMS settings, page-level meta robots tags, and HTTP X-Robots-Tag headers. Remove noindex from any pages that should be indexed. Especially check category/tag pages, product pages, and any content pages that may have been accidentally tagged.
